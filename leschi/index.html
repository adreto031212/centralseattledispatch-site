The latest in Leschi

November 12, 2026

Leschi Park, 201 Lakeside Ave S, is a rolling hillside of lawn planted with exotic trees and rose gardens, with tennis courts, a play area, restrooms and boat launches for motorboats and hand-carried boats. It is open 6 AM to 10 PM. Seattle Parks

November 5, 2026

Frink Park, 398 Lake Washington Blvd S, is a densely vegetated ravine. South Frink Place winds through it and opens onto Lake Washington Boulevard. Seattle Parks

October 29, 2026

An update on the Leschi South Marina rebuild at 100 Lakeside Avenue S: street parking on Lakeside Avenue S south of Yesler Way is now limited to 2 hours, a change made at the community's request. About 200 spaces for the public, marina tenants and park users stay open through early November, weather permitting; the southbound bus stop has moved mid-block. Night-work dates are still to be posted. Seattle Parks

October 22, 2026

Night work on Lakeside Avenue S for the Leschi South Marina rebuild may start at the end of October at the earliest; the city will post dates once they are set. Work starts in the evening, mostly in the southbound lane, with the loudest saw cutting and jackhammering done before 10 PM, so daytime parking and deliveries for the Leschi Market stay open. Seattle Parks

October 17, 2026

The $13.2 million rebuild of Leschi South Marina, 100 Lakeside Avenue S, started September 14: a new public dock and breakwater replace the old creosote dock, with a boat sewage pump-out station. Utility work on land runs three to four months. Some Lakeside Avenue work moves to nights, not before the end of October, with the loudest work done by 10 PM. The pier parking lot is closed; about 200 spaces remain in the park and along the street. Marine construction runs through spring 2027. Seattle Parks

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Central Seattle newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Central Seattle that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Central Seattle's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
