The latest in Harrison / Denny-Blaine

November 12, 2026

Howell Park, 1740 Howell Pl, is a planted trail that drops through the woods to a beach lawn on Lake Washington, with public shore access, benches and big trees. The access road is a tiny residential street with no parking, so walk in. Seattle Parks

November 5, 2026

Seattle Parks is developing a long-term site improvement plan for Denny Blaine Park, at 200 Lake Washington Blvd E, through the Park CommUNITY Fund. The work starts with a comprehensive study before any improvements are built. Seattle Parks

October 29, 2026

Lakeview Park, 340 37th Avenue E, is the lookout where Lake Washington Boulevard E starts its switchback descent to the lake: peonies against a stone wall and a view of the lake and the Cascades. Beside the lookout, a trail drops to the next boulevard loop under a coastal redwood. Seattle Parks

October 22, 2026

Viretta Park, 151 Lake Washington Boulevard E, is a grassy lookout below steep slopes, a quick stop for a look at the lake on a boulevard drive or walk. Park stairs climb from the boulevard to 39th Avenue E and E John St. Seattle Parks

October 17, 2026

Denny Blaine Park on Lake Washington is open 6 AM to 10 PM. The old stone wall around its grassy beach marks the shoreline before 1917, when building the Lake Washington Ship Canal lowered the lake nine feet. Up the hill, the RapidRide G Line on Madison Street links Madison Valley with downtown, with buses every six minutes most of the day. Seattle Parks · RapidRide G

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Central Seattle newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Central Seattle that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Central Seattle's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
