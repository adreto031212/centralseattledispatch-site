The latest in Central District

November 12, 2026

The $11.1 million Garfield Super Block renovation at 537 25th Ave is ahead of schedule on its walking paths: the west and east side pathways are expected this fall, and the play area and new restroom in early 2027. The plan adds a 0.34-mile accessible walking loop, basketball courts beside the tennis courts and Seattle Parks' first official parkour space. Seattle Parks

November 5, 2026

The city's social housing developer announced plans on Oct. 16 for a 20-home building on a 7,800-square-foot lot next to the Central District public library branch. The developer hopes to have the building open by 2028, and the project costs about $15 million. KUOW

October 29, 2026

Homer Harris Park, 2401 E Howell St, has a Unity Plaza gathering place, artwork, picnic tables, grills and lawn, with views to Lake Washington and the Cascades. It is named for Dr. Homer Harris (1916–2007), a Seattle athlete and physician, and its artwork marks highlights of his life. Seattle Parks

October 22, 2026

Garfield Community Center, 2323 E Cherry St, runs free drop-in programs this fall with a free Quick Card from the front desk: open gym Saturdays 3 to 5 PM for all ages, soul line dance Saturdays 10 AM to noon, a walking club Thursdays 9 to 10 AM, and pickleball Thursday and Friday for adults. Hours: weekdays 9 AM to 8 PM, Saturday 10 AM to 5 PM, closed Sunday. Seattle Parks

October 17, 2026

The Douglass-Truth Branch, 2300 E Yesler Way, marks 20 years this month since it was expanded and reopened on October 14, 2006; the original library building is a city landmark. On Wednesday, October 28, 1 to 3 PM, it hosts Enterprise Nexus, a session with small-business advisors and community resources. Library · Event

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Central Seattle newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Central Seattle that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Central Seattle's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
