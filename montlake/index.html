The latest in Montlake

November 12, 2026

The new westbound Portage Bay Bridge is taking shape: WSDOT crews have taken the forms off its first pair of columns near Boyer Avenue E and are working west to east, pouring more piers. WSDOT's construction map now lists Boyer Avenue E at one lane between E Roanoke St and Everett Avenue E on weekdays through Nov. 24. WSDOT · SR 520 Construction Corner

November 5, 2026

Boyer Avenue East is down to one lane between East Roanoke Street and Everett Avenue East through Nov. 20. The East Roanoke Street alley, between East Edgar Street and Boyer Avenue East, is closed Nov. 2 through 6. SR 520 Construction Corner

October 29, 2026

The Montlake Branch library, 2401 24th Avenue E, opened its current building on August 12, 2006. It is open 10 AM to 6 PM every day but Thursday, when hours run noon to 8 PM, and the book return on the E McGraw St side takes books around the clock. Seattle Public Library

October 22, 2026

Montlake Playfield, 1618 E Calhoun St, next to the Montlake Community Center, has a play area, a track, playfields and tennis courts; court reservations run through the Amy Yee Tennis Center. Nearby, the SR 520 ramp closures in this week's lead item run October 26–29. Seattle Parks

October 17, 2026

The park in this week's budget item would sit on the WSDOT Peninsula at the Arboretum's north end, with Lake Washington Boulevard realigned and the trail carried to the Montlake Lid. Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Central Seattle newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Central Seattle that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Central Seattle's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
