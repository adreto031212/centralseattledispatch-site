The latest in Madison Park

November 12, 2026

Pioneer Hall, 1642 43rd Ave E, was built in 1910 on a site donated by Judge and Mrs. John McGilvra, paid for by a $20,000 bequest from Sarah Loretta Denny. It went on the National Register of Historic Places on June 5, 1970. Seattle historical sites

November 5, 2026

Madison Park, 4201 E Madison St, has two tennis courts that you can reserve through the city's tennis court reservation page, and a bathhouse with restrooms at the swimming beach. Seattle Parks

October 29, 2026

Madison Park North Beach, 2300 43rd Avenue E at E Lynn St, sits one block north of the swim beach: a long, narrow strip of grass sloping down to Lake Washington, with a swing set and benches. A rock seawall lines the shore, so it is a place to sit and look, not to swim or launch a boat. Seattle Parks

October 22, 2026

Madison Park, 4201 E Madison St, looks east across Lake Washington to the floating bridge and the Cascades beyond. Beyond the beach, the park has two tennis courts, a play area and a big grassy slope, with shops and restaurants across the street. Seattle Parks

October 17, 2026

The park's lawn slopes down to a 400-foot beach on Lake Washington, with cement steps at the north end, a short sandy stretch at the south and a bathhouse with restrooms; lifeguards patrol it in summer. One block north, Madison Park North Beach, at E Lynn St and 43rd Avenue E, is a long, narrow strip of grass sloping down to the lake. Madison Park · North Beach

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Central Seattle newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Central Seattle that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Central Seattle's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
