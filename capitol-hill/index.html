The latest in Capitol Hill

November 12, 2026

Miller Playfield, 330 19th Ave E, sits next to the Miller Community Center, with fields for soccer, baseball and softball, tennis and basketball courts, a play area and a spray park fountain for summer. It is open 6 AM to 10 PM. Seattle Parks

November 5, 2026

Roanoke Park, 950 E Roanoke St, sits where 10th Avenue E ends on north Capitol Hill, and its play area is in line for renovation. The city has opened an engagement hub for the Roanoke Park Play Area Renovation, where you can read about the project and take part in the process. Seattle Parks

October 29, 2026

Volunteer Park, 1247 15th Avenue E, marks 15 years as a Seattle landmark on Monday, November 2; the Landmarks Preservation Board designated it on November 2, 2011. The Olmsted Brothers drew its formal plans between 1904 and 1909, and the drives, lily ponds, music pavilion and conservatory were finished by 1912. The park is open 6 AM to 10 PM, the water tower 10 AM to dusk. Seattle Parks

October 22, 2026

Cal Anderson Park, 1635 11th Avenue, sits just southeast of the Capitol Hill light rail station. It has a fountain, a texture pool and a reflecting pool, promenade paths, a shelterhouse, a plaza, a play area, tennis courts and ball fields; it is open 4 AM to 11:30 PM. Seattle Parks

October 17, 2026

The Volunteer Park Conservatory, 1400 E Galer St, has a chrysanthemum display by the Sogetsu Mercer Island Branch through Sunday, October 18, and again Tuesday to Sunday, October 20–25. The Evergreen Chrysanthemum Association Show follows, Friday, October 30, 11 AM, to Sunday, November 1, 3 PM; the club's members grow about 150 cultivars. Both come with admission ($6 adults, $4 ages 6–17). The conservatory is open 10 AM to 4 PM, closed Mondays, and free on first Thursdays; the next is November 5. Calendar · Plan your visit

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Central Seattle newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Central Seattle that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Central Seattle's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
