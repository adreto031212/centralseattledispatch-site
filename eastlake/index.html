The latest in Eastlake

November 12, 2026

RapidRide J Line crews are installing curb ramps, new sidewalk and electrical wiring at E Allison, E Hamlin, E Roanoke, E Lynn and E Newton streets along Eastlake Avenue E. Short sections of sidewalk close while they work, with a marked temporary path around them, and the businesses at all of these corners stay open. SDOT

November 5, 2026

The water main work for the RapidRide J Line on Eastlake Avenue E is finished. Crews installed more than 9,000 feet of new main, 27 new fire hydrants and 174 new connections to homes and businesses, and SDOT calls it the last major water upgrade in the area. SDOT

October 29, 2026

RapidRide J Line crews may start a new sidewalk at the southwest corner of E Lynn St and Eastlake Avenue E as early as Monday, November 2. Driveway paving continues, mostly on the east side between E Louisa St and E Lynn St, and the retaining wall between E Allison St and Harvard Avenue E is close to done. The project's online open house is Thursday, November 5, 2 to 3 PM. SDOT

October 22, 2026

RapidRide J Line crews start on the southeast corner of Eastlake Avenue E and E Louisa St as early as Monday, October 26, digging up sidewalk toward E Franklin St. The alley entrance on E Louisa closes for the work; use the E Lynn St entrance. Crews are also starting the southbound station near E Garfield St and building a retaining wall between E Allison St and Harvard Avenue E. SDOT

October 17, 2026

RapidRide J Line crews are paving Eastlake Avenue E in front of driveways and pouring new driveway aprons, some of it on weekends, with sidewalk and electrical work at E Hamlin, E Roanoke, E Louisa and E Newton streets. Right turns from Fairview Avenue N onto southbound Eastlake are restricted during construction; the way around is north on Fairview Avenue E, right on E Blaine St, then right onto Eastlake. Route 70 becomes RapidRide in 2027. SDOT

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Central Seattle newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Central Seattle that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Central Seattle's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
